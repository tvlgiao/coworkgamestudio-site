Coffee Street: Dalat Hills, our next game

By Cowork Game Studio · Published 29 October 2026 · Coffee Street: Dalat Hills

Da Lat sits high in Viet Nam's Central Highlands, a town of pine forests, flower gardens and cool mornings where a cup of coffee is part of the day. Our next game starts there, with a small coffee cart on a grassy hillside.

Coffee Street: Dalat Hills is a cozy isometric game about running that cart and slowly turning the hill around it into a place people want to visit. Its store name is Vietnam Cloudhill & Pines Cafe; on the devlog we keep calling it Coffee Street. When we first wrote this post it was an early prototype, but the whole loop already played from start to finish, so we wanted to share a first look. The game has moved a long way since then, so scroll down for the November update.

Brew, serve, build

Passers-by stroll along the path and stop at your cart. You brew their drinks (three of them in the first prototype) and serve them while they wait. What you earn goes back into the hill around you:

  • place tables and stools so customers can sit down,
  • plant flowers and pines,
  • draw stone paths and fences wherever you like,
  • hire a barista to help at the cart.

Up the hill

As the cart gets busier, milestones open up more of the hill. Wooden stairs lead up to a higher terrace, where you can open a scenic deck among the pines. After the milestones the game turns into a free sandbox, so you can keep building the hillside at your own pace.

What you were looking at

In October everything in these pictures was generated in code: simple shapes and flat colours that let us test the game before the final art. We said the look would change and the feeling would not. Both turned out true. The goal is still a slow, friendly game about a coffee cart, a garden and a view.

Update, November 2026

Since this post the whole story has come together, and we wrote about that in the whole story is playable. This update goes one level deeper: how the world is put together, how guests and staff behave, how saves work and what is landing next.

A world built by code, finished by hand

The game runs on Unity 6.3 LTS with the Universal Render Pipeline. The scene file itself holds almost nothing: a single bootstrap object. When the game starts, code generates the lots, paths, terraces, terrain and most of the props. Every colour comes from one shared palette, so the hill, the furniture and the interface stay in the same family.

Each object is split in two. The gameplay root carries the colliders, the anchor points guests walk to and the logic. Under it sits one visual child, which is only the look. That split is why the simple shapes from October could be swapped out without touching a line of gameplay. The furniture, counters, flower greenhouse, koi pond and decoration sets are now hand-finished 3D models that slot in as new visual children.

Each model is baked into High, Medium and Low tiers, and a phone loads only the tier it needs. The pictures in the build catalog are pre-rendered when we make a build, instead of being drawn when you open the menu. On a Mac, the first open of the build menu went from about 0.7 seconds to 0.08 seconds.

A day on the hill

A day has three parts: morning, opening the shop, and evening. Morning mist hangs over the valley. After dusk the lamps, lanterns and string lights come on. The simulation keeps its own clock, separate from the interface, so pausing freezes every guest and staff member in place while the menus keep responding.

Guests who choose their seat

Guests are navigation agents that walk the hill on their own. They queue at the cart, order, then carry their own cup to a seat. They do not pick the nearest free stool. Each guest scores the seats around them by the view, the shade, nearby flowers and lamps, and walks to the one they like best.

Tourists stop at scenic spots to take photos, and some of those photos come back to you as postcards. Regulars return day after day. Each regular has a story of their own, and over time they fill a guest book.

Decorate for joy

This is the third rule in our design doc, and it shaped a lot of the other systems. Decorations are not income multipliers. A flower bed does not add a percentage to your earnings. Instead, decorations change where guests sit and what tourists photograph. A table under a lamp, next to flowers, with a view down the valley, is the table guests head for.

We made that choice because multipliers push players toward the most profitable grid, and that is not what a cafe on a Da Lat hillside should look like. We want you to build the cafe you find beautiful, not the one with the best numbers.

Staff

  • Baristas brew on their own, a few seconds per drink.
  • A server carries drinks out, so guests can sit down straight away instead of waiting at the cart.
  • A guitarist plays in the evening and lifts the mood of the tables nearby.

Wages are paid daily. If you cannot cover a day's wages, that person takes the day off. There is no debt to dig out of.

Saves

The game saves automatically in the background. Each save is written in one atomic step with a backup copy kept alongside, so a crash in the middle of a save cannot corrupt your cafe. Saves stay on your device and are included in your phone's own backup. In the browser version they stay in the browser. Coming soon: up to three separate cafes, each with its own progress, sharing the same settings and language.

The story and after

The story runs over five chapters: 1, the coffee cart at the foot of the slope; 2, a deck above the valley; 3, the little pine grove; 4, the flower greenhouse, with a koi pond; and 5, the best café on the hill, a hilltop with a sunset deck, a telescope and a stage. It ends with the Hill Coffee Festival: the regulars gather on the hilltop, Tùng sings and paper fireworks open over the valley.

After the festival, an endless mode keeps going: lots to buy along the street, street levels, seasonal drinks, a daily request board, roast upgrades and a new decoration set for each street level.

Landing next

  • Hillside terraces along the whole street, with lots of equal size. You buy wooden stairs and place them yourself to reach each terrace.
  • Lots you can't buy yet stay hidden; lots you can buy show dimmed until you do.
  • Themed street lots get their own 3D scenery: a maple grove, a tea hill, a vegetable garden and a pink grass meadow.
  • Koi in the pond swim their own paths and leave ripples when they rise.

Controls and languages

On PC, Mac and the web you can play with mouse, keyboard or a controller. On phones you play by touch or with a controller. The game is in English and Vietnamese.
